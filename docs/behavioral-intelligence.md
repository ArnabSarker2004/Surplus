# Behavioral and economic intelligence

The implementation compares the user's own observed activity, with missing-data gates and editable purchase context. It does not classify users as impulsive, infer psychological traits, or treat population research as a personal diagnosis.

## Methods and evidence thresholds

| Feature                           | Implemented method                                                                                                                   | Minimum evidence / limits                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Weekday optional-spending prompts | Complete-week category averages, including zero-spend days                                                                           | Four complete weeks; purchases in at least three and half the observed weeks; average at least $8 and 1.4× the other-weekday average. Groceries, income, transfers and unreviewed purchases are excluded. Routine labels override optional classification.                             |
| Purchase frequency                | Shrunk descriptive rates, then constant vs weekday Poisson regression                                                                | Eight complete weeks and 30 optional purchases; at least 15 training events. The final 14 days are held out. A weekday model must reduce Poisson deviance by 5% versus a constant rate. Selected model refits all observed complete weeks. No calibrated impulse probability is shown. |
| Hour windows / purchase sequences | Three-hour weekday windows; consecutive different-category purchases within two hours                                                | Genuine timezone-aware authorization timestamps; three distinct days. Converted to America/Toronto with IANA tzdata. No posting-time substitute or synthetic timestamps. Counts are associations, not causal triggers.                                                                 |
| Where purchases happen            | City/region/country aggregates and merchant counts                                                                                   | Three visits per reported city; online transactions excluded. No addresses, coordinates, home/work labels or external geocoding. Timestamp/location coverage is shown explicitly.                                                                                                      |
| Payday comparison                 | Optional spending on payday plus two days vs the same weekdays one week earlier                                                      | Four non-overlapping cycles. Control days near another paycheck are excluded. Employment deposits only. This controls weekday composition, not seasonality, changing plans or every confounder.                                                                                        |
| Category price scenario           | Published Ontario/Canada monthly CPI and spending-weighted category rates                                                            | A current published series and same-month year-ago observation; latest period within 120 days. Annual rates bounded to -5%…20%. Public API failures fall back to a labeled cache or no adjustment.                                                                                     |
| Merchant ticket trend             | Median charge in the last 90 days vs the preceding 90 days                                                                           | Four purchases in each window, change of at least 10%. **Not personal inflation**: receipt quantities, item prices, tips and basket composition are unknown. Does not feed the CPI price estimate.                                                                                     |
| Rising recurring charges          | Monthly interval regularity plus daily-event autocorrelation at lags 26–35 days                                                      | Six charges; 80% interval regularity; last charge within 45 days; autocorrelation at least .2; last-three median exceeds first-three by $1 and 5%. No claim that the service is unused.                                                                                                |
| Near-term liquidity               | 7/14-day statistics from the existing 400 cash-flow paths                                                                            | Cash balance and at least 28 observed days / three complete weeks for simulation shares. Minimum median cash, buffer/zero breach shares and projected income dates are shown. These are combined-cash estimates, not per-account overdraft probabilities.                              |
| Optional one-time set-aside       | 10th percentile of each path's minimum balance through the selected horizon, less buffer, saved goals and all remaining goal targets | Requires usable history/cash and a non-stale supplied bank snapshot; floored at zero. Protects the entire horizon, not just the next month. It is never an instruction to transfer that amount every day.                                                                              |
| Card comparison                   | Existing deterministic reward rules, category caps, fees and repayment assumptions                                                   | Recommendations are withheld for unknown/carried repayment status, stale terms, insufficient history, or unavailable/stale/pressured near-term liquidity. Estimates remain visible for comparison.                                                                                     |

The constant-rate baseline is preferable when weekday features do not improve held-out predictions. Hawkes fitting is deliberately not claimed: sparse timestamps and weekly schedules can masquerade as self-excitation. Likewise, no logistic or Cox model is claimed without actual daily balance histories and observed breach labels. The simulation-based liquidity view is usable today without inventing those training labels.

## Dynamic goals and interventions

Every forecast allocates one shared cash pool across goals in priority/deadline order while protecting the lowest remaining path balance. The goal view shows weekly median/range allocations, what-if paths, funding dates, and date changes since the previous comparable snapshot. Higher spending can delay a date; additional income or a lower discretionary-spending assumption can bring it forward. An allocation is affordable capacity, not an executed bank transfer.

The habits view can apply a 25% category/weekday reduction to a temporary scenario. This reduction acts on variable spending only and is applied after general flexible-spending reductions to avoid double counting. The UI opens goal timelines with the changed path. Users can mark a specific purchase routine/planned and undo that correction; those amounts still count in total spending. Saved budget limits never change on a forecast read.

The CPI panel exposes current modeled monthly category baselines and a same-quantity 12-month price illustration. Price-indexed forecasts are dynamic; saved spending caps remain decisions for the user. Repeated-merchant ticket changes are shown separately because the app has no item-level receipts to establish personal price inflation.

## Public data and research

- [Statistics Canada table 18-10-0004-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1810000401) provides monthly, not seasonally adjusted CPI. The [Web Data Service](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/en/developers/wds/user-guide) adapter requests fixed Ontario/Canada coordinates for all-items, groceries, restaurants, household, clothing, public transport, health and recreation. Broad shopping uses all-items, not an assumed clothing basket. Data is monthly, not real-time, and carrying the latest annual change forward is a scenario, not an official forecast.
- [Stephens, Paycheck Receipt and the Timing of Consumption](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9356) motivates examining spending timing around income. The implementation reports within-person associations and does not import a population percentage into a user's score.
- [Carvalho, Meier and Wang, Poverty and Economic Decision-Making](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5167530/) reports mixed evidence around payday and cognitive mechanisms. It does not justify inferring a person's self-control from account transactions.
- [Filimonov and Sornette, Hawkes calibration issues](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6756) motivates caution about apparent clustering, nonstationarity and timestamp quality. This is methodological evidence from financial event data, not a validated consumer impulse model.
- [Plaid Transactions API](https://plaid.com/docs/api/products/transactions/) distinguishes authorization time from posting time and explains that location coverage varies. Only provided metadata is retained. The first sync after this update replays the existing Item's transaction stream once to hydrate metadata previously discarded. Pages, cursor and metadata version commit atomically; failed hydration keeps prior financial data and cursor. No new Item, relink, new product or invented timestamp is involved.

## Operations and privacy

Authorization dates and times already supplied by the bank survive pending-to-posted reconciliation when the posted record omits them, including pending removals delivered on an earlier sync page. Reuse requires the same source/account and a matching transaction ID or Plaid pending link. A newly supplied timestamp takes precedence. Posting dates never substitute for missing purchase times.

No transaction data is sent to Statistics Canada or a research site. Express caches public CPI separately under `data/public-data/`; Python receives a validated economic context alongside its minimal private financial payload. No new paid service, LLM key, geolocation permission or payment integration is needed. Monthly CPI has release lag, and cached or absent data is explicitly labeled. Demo CPI snapshots are public data, while the transaction fixture remains fictional.

The app offers in-app planning prompts. There is no SMS/email scheduler, push notification, HISA account opening, automatic sweep or bank transfer. No population model is trained across accounts. Development tests use synthetic transactions; the browser suite checks the real Python integration, every view at 360/768/1440 widths, SVG tick bounds, accessibility, corrections, retries and goal navigation.
